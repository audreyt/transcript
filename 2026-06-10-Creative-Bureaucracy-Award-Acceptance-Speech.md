# 2026-06-10 Creative Bureaucracy Award Acceptance Speech

### Audrey Tang:

Five thousand years. Stylus on clay.<br>
The first writing was bookkeeping:<br>
shared grain, counted and kept<br>
a village alive through winter.

The first writer was a bureaucrat.<br>
The empires are dust.<br>
The ledgers remain.

Clay is soil that remembers.<br>
So is data:<br>
only alive when many hands work it,<br>
richer with every harvest.

Till the data soil.<br>
Don’t drill for data oil.

Sovereignty that rests<br>
on one good officeholder<br>
is not yet sovereignty. It is luck.<br>
In Taiwan, we planted ours:<br>
a commons no single hand can uproot.

They say bureaucracy moves slowly.<br>
So do tectonic plates.<br>
Patient pressure<br>
raised our islands<br>
from the sea.

Now the ledger counts voices.<br>
Four hundred and forty-seven citizens —<br>
our islands in miniature —<br>
deliberated on deepfakes, face to face.

Civil servants carried<br>
what the citizens chose<br>
into law, and made the law hold.

In one year,<br>
impersonation scam ads fell<br>
by ninety-four per cent.

We work with the people,<br>
not just for the people.<br>
With the government,<br>
not just for the government.

You have a beautiful phrase for us:<br>
“Creative Bureaucrats.”<br>
The oldest writers,<br>
still writing.

This award is not mine alone.<br>
Somewhere tonight<br>
a civil servant is still at the desk:<br>
working in the open,<br>
keeping the winter out.

The granary holds,<br>
because of you.

Thank you.
